<h2><strong>Does Green Tea Boost Testosterone?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The best recent green tea testosterone data I could read is a study in </span><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Frontiers in Public Health</span></em><span style="font-weight: 400;">, published February 25, 2025, that compared long-term green tea drinkers with non-drinkers.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">According to the</span><a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1531953/full"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">study</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, it included 280 middle-aged and older men in rural Hubei, China. Median testosterone was 574.66 ng/dL in the tea group and 495.89 ng/dL in the control group.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Estradiol and thyroid hormones did not differ significantly.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So, does green tea increase testosterone? This study says tea drinkers had higher levels, but the authors note limits:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Groups were defined by existing habits, not randomly assigned.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Tea intake wasn't measured by amount, frequency or concentration.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Only middle-aged and older men were studied.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Other foods and drinks weren't controlled.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That means the finding can't tell you that green tea caused higher testosterone.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For the question "does green tea kill testosterone" or "does green tea lower testosterone," the same study points the other way, though one study isn't the last word.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I also saw a published trial title reporting that green tea catechin extract did not change circulating sex hormones in postmenopausal women at high breast cancer risk.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I could only see the title, so I'm not relying on its details.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Green Tea Benefits for Men</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Setting testosterone aside, what are the green tea benefits for men?&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Harvard Health (February 1, 2025) says tea drinking is linked in observational research with lower risks of heart disease, high blood pressure, stroke, diabetes and early death, and it suggests two to four cups a day of green, black or oolong tea.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It calls the evidence promising but not conclusive.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Harvard Nutrition Source adds that very hot tea is linked to higher esophageal and stomach cancer risk, and that caffeine can affect sleep and heart rate.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Green tea does not balance hormones in any proven way, so if you ask "does green tea balance hormones," the careful answer is: not shown.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Unusually low energy, low sex drive or mood changes deserve a blood test and a conversation with a doctor.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Supplements and Herbs That Claim to Raise Testosterone</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many men look at the best vitamins for testosterone, herbs that boost testosterone and the question of what vitamins boost testosterone.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A 2019 summary from the Sexual Medicine Society of North America (SMSNA), dated August 13, 2019, reviewed a study of 50 "T booster" products. Per the</span><a href="https://smsna.org/patients/news/testosterone-supplement-claims-not-always-backed-by-medical-research"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">SMSNA report</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Over 60% of the products had no scientific research supporting their claims.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Only 5.5% had more than two medical studies on testosterone effects.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ninety percent claimed to boost testosterone, but only 24.8% of those had data to back it.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">About a quarter of ingredients were linked to higher testosterone, 18% to no change and 10% to lower levels.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Two products contained more zinc than the FDA's upper tolerable intake level.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I could not verify individual claims here, such as whether selenium increases testosterone or whether any specific vitamin works.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So does selenium increase testosterone? I found no evidence I could read, and the SMSNA piece warns that claims often outrun the data.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ask your doctor to test for deficiency before you take anything, since extra zinc and other nutrients can cause harm.</span></p>

<h2><strong>When to See a Doctor About Low Testosterone</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Symptoms of low testosterone are not unique to it, and many other conditions cause fatigue and low drive.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A doctor can order tests and review your medicines and sleep. Prescription testosterone has its own risks.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A February 2024 editorial in the Cleveland Clinic Journal of Medicine notes that the 2020 American College of Physicians guideline found low-certainty evidence of a modest benefit on sexual function, and that the TRAVERSE trial saw more arrhythmias, including atrial fibrillation, and thromboembolic events.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That is another reason to get a proper diagnosis before chasing any booster.</span></p>
